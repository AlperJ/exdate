01 · The problem
What happens when AAPLx pays

Apple has paid five dividends on Solana. Nothing told the people who held it.

There is no transfer. The token count is identical before and after. What moves is a multiplier stored on the token, and every holder's balance is worth more the instant the issuer nudges it.

No wallet surfaces it. No explorer records it. No portfolio tracker accounts for it. You can hold Apple through AAPLx for a year, be paid five times, and have no way to find out.

02 · Why nothing shows it
AAPLx, read from Solana mainnet

The obvious field is the stale one, and reading it fails without an error.

multiplierstale 1.0026642075893797
newMultiplier 1.0032690125398187

newMultiplier takes over the moment newMultiplierEffectiveTimestamp passes, and the chain never rewrites the old field. On a cash dividend the error is 0.06%. On NFLXx after its ten-for-one split, an app reading the obvious field shows a tenth of the real balance.

03 · What it adds up to
Paid to holders with no transaction, as of 13 September 2026
$11,984,613

Across 624 payments on 325 tokenized stocks, out of 832 issued on Solana. 628 payments across 329 stocks reached a holder; four of those stocks had no usable price, so they are counted and left out of the money. Every figure read from the chain and from the issuer’s own records.

04 · How big this actually is

It is not an xStocks problem. The second issuer is larger than the first.

Backed / xStocks — 832 assets $608.3m
Holders, xStocks alone, +83% in 30 days 300,000+

We did not take Ondo’s word for it. Their repository says GM tokens carry the ScaledUiAmount extension, so we read the mint. AAPLon, Ondo’s Apple, carries a multiplier of 1.003376073740221. Apple’s other tokenized form, AAPLx, sits at 1.003269012539819. Same company, two tokens, two different accumulated dividend factors, and nothing compares them.

Roughly 1,200 mints and $1.4bn already pay this way on Solana, every one of them invisible to its holders in the same manner. rwa.xyz, 13 September 2026.

05 · What we found measuring it

Three things the numbers only show once you join them to a holder.

900%
A split is not a dividend.

Of 654 recorded corporate actions, 641 are dividends and 13 are splits or corrections, and 651 of the 654 have actually taken effect. Count them all as income and the total reads $14.3m instead of $12.0m — 19% too much. But the error is not spread across the market: it lands entirely on the twelve stocks that split. Netflix went from 1.0 to 10.0 and holders received nothing, so a tracker reading that as income tells a Netflix holder they gained 900% on a position worth exactly what it was.

38 / 539
The issuer's forward calendar is 93% stale.

It serves 539 rows as upcoming. 482 already activated, 14 are the same event listed twice, and 5 carry no date at all — and an undated row is the dangerous one, because +new Date(null) is zero and it sorts silently into the distant past. 38 rows are genuinely ahead. Build a calendar off that endpoint without checking the clock and your number is fourteen times too large.

90%
The dividend market is one instrument.

Nine tenths of the total is STRCx, a variable-rate preferred that pays like a bond. The other 324 stocks come to $1.2m between them, and across all 325 priced payers the typical one has paid 0.370% of its own value since launch. Tokenized equity dividends are tiny — which is exactly why nobody noticed they were invisible.

06 · The product
Live at exdate-ten.vercel.app

Three things you can do, right now, without connecting anything.

Paste a wallet

Get a statement: what was paid, when, per holding, with the multiplier change behind every payment.

Type a ticker

See what that stock has paid since launch, when the next payment lands, the tax withheld, and whether the tokens are really backed by shares in custody.

Read the calendar

Every payment still to come, filtered against the clock on every request, with the date each one activates.

Entirely read-only. No wallet connection, no transaction, no custody, no contract. It reads public state and states what it found.

07 · Why Solana

The mechanism barely exists off Solana.

Paying a dividend by moving a multiplier on the token is a Token-2022 extension. Its EVM analogue, ERC-8056, runs mainly on Robinhood Chain, where Scallar already indexes it. Almost every tokenized equity that pays this way is on Solana, so the problem is Solana-native, the fix has to be, and every byte of data needed to build it is already public.

08 · What a prize builds

The site proves the problem. The fix is not a website.

The correct reader, so the fix travels — 0–3 months

An MIT package that resolves the multiplier against its effective timestamp, tells a dividend from a split by the issuer’s own reason field, and rebuilds the balance a wallet held on any past date. Ours showing these payments helps only the people who find us; one integration in Phantom, Solflare, Backpack or Step reaches more holders than this site ever will. It makes the hard half free for everyone, including the competitor.

Tell people before it happens — 1–4 months

Every payment is knowable a day ahead: the issuer publishes it and the change is staged on chain with a future effective timestamp. Email, Telegram, and a webhook apps can subscribe to. This is what turns a lookup into something people come back to.

The statement nobody can produce — 2–6 months

Per wallet, per tax year: date, event, multiplier before and after, balance held that day, tokens received, value, withholding already applied. The clock on this is regulatory, not commercial. 1099-DA cost-basis reporting begins with transactions on or after 1 January 2026, first forms early 2027.

Every issuer, one record — 4–9 months

Ondo first, because it is the larger set and uses the identical extension. Then Backpack, then whoever is next. The reader from phase one makes each new issuer a configuration rather than a rewrite.

The holders who are not in a wallet — 6–12 months

On-chain is only 14–20% of cumulative xStocks volume, so the majority bought on Kraken or Bybit, where an address lookup finds nothing. Whether those venues pass the dividend through, and whether they tell the customer, is undocumented. Finding out is journalism as much as engineering.

09 · How it pays for itself

This category already has a price. We are the expensive half of it.

LSEG publishes a corporate-actions rate card: £31,100 a year for one ISO 15022 data file, and £8,685 to £64,200 for the right to redistribute it, scaling with how many customers you put it in front of. That ladder is the exact shape of a wallet integration.

And the feed is the cheap part. ISSA puts data sourcing at 56% of the total cost of processing a corporate action, and says procuring the data costs less than “interpretations, cleansing, enrichment”. A corporate-action unit costs $3–5m a year to run, and over 45% of brokers have taken a $1m+ loss from a processing error. We do not sell a feed. We sell the reconciled result.

Free, permanently — $0

Any address, any holder, any judge. No sign-in, no wallet connection. Plus the open-source reader. A lookup is a handful of RPC reads, so this stays cheap.

The statement — $29–49 per wallet-year

The dated, exportable, filing-ready record. Every comparable product gives tracking away and charges for the artifact: Koinly, CoinLedger, CoinTracker and Awaken all land between $29 and $99 per tax year.

Integration — $200–1,000/mo

Machine-readable access, freshness guarantees, and the right to show the result to your own users. Helius, Birdeye and Shyft all price in this band.

Redistribution — quoted, on LSEG’s ladder

Exchanges, custodians, issuers and funds: bulk history, event webhooks, an audit trail, and a signed artifact they can hand a regulator.

Pyth proved the model at Solana speed: core feeds free forever, $500/mo to display, $2,500/mo to redistribute, and $10.4m ARR within eleven months. The fence is the licence, not the numbers.

10 · What it is not

Stated plainly, because the whole product is a claim about accuracy.

It is read-only. It tells you what happened; it does not let you act on it.
Dollar figures value the tokens the issuer reports as circulating — its own published number, covering every chain a token is issued on.
Dividends arrive as extra tokens, not cash, so dollar figures value that growth at today's price and move with the stock.
Wallet figures use the balance the wallet actually held on each payment date, read from the last transaction before it. Where an account trades too often for that walk to reach back, the row is labelled estimated and the headline says how many.
Before submitting, adversarial reviewers were told to prove our numbers false by routes the app does not use. They confirmed most and broke several. Every one is fixed and written up, including the one where our own headline was seven times too high, and the one where every wallet figure was priced against today’s balance instead of the balance held on the day.
We are not the first, and one of them is close. SolanaRWA has been turning these same multiplier changes into per-holder dividend income since May 2026, on this chain and this token extension. Lido has let anyone paste an Ethereum address and read their rebase history for five years. Dinari keeps per-account dividend records on EVM chains behind KYC. We searched twice, and we name them ourselves.
The events themselves are the issuer's, published free and already labelled dividend or split. We did not discover them and we do not claim them. What we could not find is the backward half: SolanaRWA compares each refresh against its last snapshot, so it sees payments from the day you sign up, for a wallet you connect and control — its own writing calls reconstructing the history instead “possible but more complex”. Paste any address here, with no sign-in, and read what it was already paid, against the balance it actually held on each date, with splits kept separate from dividends.
